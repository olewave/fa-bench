# TIMIT — detailed results

Every field the scorer emits, grouped by the pairing each metric belongs to.
The headline comparison — MAE and F1, clean and noisy — is in
[README](README.md).

Three things nest in each table: the split, the metric group, and the
condition. Groups are not commensurable and are ruled apart accordingly:
label-paired metrics pair phones by identity and are blind to a system that
skips hard ones; time-paired metrics pair by clock and are blind to labels.

<!-- BEGIN GENERATED: caption-timit -->
**MAE**: mean absolute boundary error, on the matched path only.
**P/R, F1**: boundary detection at 20 ms, paired by TIME and ignoring labels — a substitution at the right time is free here.
**OS**: over-segmentation, n_hyp/n_gold − 1: positive means more boundaries proposed than exist.
**R-val**: R-value (Räsänen et al. 2009), which separates the over- from under-segmentation that F1 scores identically.
**x̃**: median absolute error — the typical boundary, unmoved by a few catastrophic ones.
**δ̄**: mean *signed* error: negative is early, positive late. Direction, where MAE and x̃ give only magnitude.
**t=N**: share of boundaries within N ms.
**95% CI**: bootstrap confidence interval over utterances.
**S / D / I**: substitutions, deletions, insertions: why a unit left the matched path — relabelled, never emitted, or invented. Normalised by gold count. Phones in the phone tables, words in the track-2 one.
**PER**: phone error rate, S + D + I.
<!-- END GENERATED: caption-timit -->

## Word-level

The comparison page carries word MAE and F1, clean against a single *Noisy*
mean. Here each condition has its own column, and precision and recall — which
the comparison page does not carry — sit beside the F1 that summarises them.

Word boundaries are matched at the same 20 ms tolerance as phones, by the same
time-based, label-agnostic matcher — so precision, recall, F1, OS and R-value
mean here exactly what they mean one tier down, counted over word boundaries
instead of phone boundaries.

### TIMIT phone detection @20 ms · segmentation balance by condition

MAE is computed on the matched path, so a system that loses phones under noise
drops them from its own average; these charge for exactly those. Precision and
recall say whether the surviving boundaries are right, OS and R-value whether
the right *number* was emitted — F1 scores over- and under-segmentation
identically, and those two are what separate them.

**OS is often flat across conditions, and that is correct.** A forced aligner
is handed the transcript, so the number of boundaries it emits is fixed by the
input; OS moves only when a system actually drops phones. Identical values
down a row mean the system emitted the same count under noise, not that the
cells are duplicates.
